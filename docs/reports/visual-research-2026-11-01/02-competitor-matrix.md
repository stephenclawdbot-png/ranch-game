## C. What the matrix says about Post Time's position

1. **The visual lane Post Time occupies (pixel art, side-on, management-first) has exactly one
   current competitor**: Sheep Meadow Road's *Horse Racing Manager* (Steam EA, Oct 2025). Its
   praise is coat genetics and attachment to "pixel horses"; its criticism is run animation
   ("awkwardly limping"), horses that "look like ponies", spread-out finishes and a clunky UI. Post
   Time is already ahead on run animation (12-frame HD gallop with normal maps), close finishes
   (line-scan photo, engine 1.4) and broadcast craft; it must **not** drift toward that game's look
   (do not adopt its saddle-cloth/number style literally; see 08 for an owned treatment).
2. **3D realism is a different lane** (Rival Stars, Photo Finish LIVE, Winning Post, Star Stable).
   Competing on polygon realism is neither affordable nor necessary; the evidence (03) is that
   players forgive stylisation but not *sameness* (one body for every breed, one mane for every
   horse) or a camera that hides or sickens.
3. **Nobody in the genre turns coat genetics into a visible ritual at the foal reveal.** Horse
   Reality shows the pattern prominently on foals; Rival Stars and Equestrian put coats behind
   currency. Post Time's coat audit already gives it the deepest *rendered* coat system of the
   pixel-art games; presenting inheritance is the open space.
4. **Watched-race presentation fatigue is real**: Winning Post's own players say the race cameras
   are watched "only at first". Post Time's skip/finish-only and verdict-first result page are the
   right answer; spend presentation budget on the last 200 m, the photo and the owner's moment,
   not on more camera angles.
