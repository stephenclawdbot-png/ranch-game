# Make Post Time a Ladder, Not a Casino

Post Time will last longest if it keeps real money out of the competitive loop and makes the loop itself the reward. The daily allowance of entries should feed a weekly qualify-or-retry funnel, the funnel should feed four-week seasons, and all of it should sit on a forgiving ladder and an economy that removes horses as fast as breeding adds them. Most of the parts already exist. What's missing is what joins them up. Players need race conditions announced days ahead so they can breed and train for a target, several ways into each Feature, a consolation final for near-misses, floors in the lower leagues, activity rules at the top, hard breeding caps, a monthly report on credits created and destroyed, and calculators for the players who like to work things out. Spend the $1,000 on one free, owner-funded "Founders' Season" with fixed prizes announced in advance, paid in ordinary money to the top 8 finishers, with ID checks only for winners. That keeps it under the US thresholds for state registration and tax forms. Do not build USDC-priced horse markets or paid-entry USDC prize races. That is the model Zed Run ran: about 9 of 12 entrants lost money in every paid race, and horses were listed from about $0.20 after it shut down ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/316789/ethereum-nft-game-zed-run-reborn-base)). US law firms say prize pools funded by entry fees look most like wagering ([Fasthoff Law](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/skill-contests-versus-sweepstakes-entry-fees)), a well-funded rival (Sorare) has been under UK prosecution since 2024 ([BlockchainGamer.biz](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/34595/uk-gambling-commission-charges-sorare/)), and the plan would break the project's own rule that Stable Credits are never cashed out. The games in this genre that have lasted, such as Hattrick (29 years), Umamusume's Champions Meeting and GT7's Daily Races, run on scheduled stakes and prestige, not payouts.

**How to read this report.** Facts about other games, data and law carry a source link. Recommendations, and sentences tagged *[inferred]*, are design judgements drawn from those sources. None of them has been tested in Post Time. Legal points describe the general picture and are **not legal advice**. Money-transmission and stablecoin payout rules, Quebec contest rules and EU consumer-promotion rules were not researched.

## The plan on one page: five loops, one ladder, no cash-out

The table below is the recommended plan, mapped to features Post Time already has. The sections after it give the reasoning and evidence for each row.

| Layer | Cadence | What the player does | Already built | Recommended change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily grind | Daily, 15–30 min over 2–5 short visits | Spends 10 entries on Live races, runs the Daily Cup, does care and training | Live race every 3 min, entry bank 10/day (holds 20), 3 starts per horse per day, 40-start careers, Daily Cup | A weekly A/B/C Live race card; League score counts only your best results, still capped at 240; entry upgrades never sold for cash |
| Weekly funnel | Mon–Fri qualifiers, Saturday Feature | Tries to qualify, fails, tries again the next day | Listed weekday qualifiers; Saturday G1 needs a top-3 in that week's qualifiers or a rating of 100+ | Conditions published Monday; four ways in; best 3 of 5 qualifier results count; a Saturday "Plate" consolation race for near-misses |
| Season | 4 weeks | Builds season points toward Season Finals | Season Finals per distance division | Featured condition each season; several ways in; A and B finals; standings reset but stables never do; a juvenile division for foals born this season |
| Ladder | Weekly promotion and relegation | Climbs tiers | Leagues of 30, Bronze to Champion, top 5 up, bottom 5 down | No relegation out of Bronze or Silver; groups balanced by a hidden stable rating; activity rule and a numbered rank in Champion |
| Economy | Monthly report | Breeds, sells, retires | Credits, appraisal floors and ceilings, 4 lifetime breedings, Legacy Farm, fatal injuries | Bring purses back to about 1/3 of today's level; tax on market sales; per-season breeding cap; stop credit trading of premium foals |
| Integrity | Continuous | Nothing; it runs in the background | Committed seeds, verifiable replays | Link accounts by identity; 1 horse per stable per qualifier field; published penalty ladder and appeals; staff trading rules |
| $1,000 | One season | Competes for free | Small beta prize pool | A "Founders' Season": free entry, fixed prizes announced in advance, fiat payout, ID check for winners only |
| USDC | Gated, not before 6+ months | Nothing yet | ETH Premium packs (beta) | No USDC horse market, no paid-entry pools; listed conditions to meet before reconsidering |

## The daily grind matters when every entry counts toward the week

Post Time's daily budget is in the normal range for the genre. Umamusume's daily ladder, Team Trials, gives about 12 attempts a day: 5 bars of race points, one refilling every 2 hours ([Deltia's Gaming](https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=281576)). Horse Racing Manager has run a live PvP race every five minutes since 2018 and is still updated in 2026 ([Skich](https://skich.app/games/horse-racing-manager-2018)). Post Time's 10 entries and 3-minute Live race sit between the two. A 15–30 minute day also matches the benchmarks: top-quarter mobile games get **22–24 minutes of play per day**, and the median PC session is 18 minutes ([GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). The risk is not the volume. The risk is that each race feels throwaway. Zed Run ran races "often several per minute" ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)), and the management sims point the other way. Hattrick plays about two matches a week, Sokker two league matches a week, Trophy Manager three, and community-run Football Manager and OOTP leagues settle on 2–4 sims a week ([Hattrick beginners' guide](https://www.hattrick.org/en/Help/BeginnersGuide.aspx); [Sokker rules](https://sokker.org/rules/action/short/chapter/12); [Trophy Manager](https://trophymanager.com/ir/); [OOTP forums](https://forums.ootpdevelopments.com/showthread.php?p=2722061)). The lesson *[inferred]* is that frequent Live races should work as the practice and qualifying layer, and every entry should visibly move a weekly number.

GT7's Daily Races show how to make frequent races meaningful. All ranked players are funnelled into **three fixed races (A, B, C) that change every Monday**. Each can be entered as often as you like. Race A usually doesn't affect your rating, so it works as an on-ramp ([GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/gran-turismo-7-daily-races-20250915); [gran-turismo.com](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/news/00_1608923.html)). Post Time should do the same with its 480 daily Live slots. Each Monday, publish a three-lane card. Lane A is unrated and open to any horse, for newcomers and testing. Lane B uses this week's Feature conditions (distance and going). Lane C uses an alternate test, such as a different distance division. Rotate Live slots across the three lanes and use sister courses for overflow. Each lane then has full fields, results within a lane can be compared, and a breeder knows on Monday what Saturday rewards *[inferred]*.

Next, make the League score reward quality, not volume. Pokémon's championship circuit counts only a player's best N finishes per event type ("Best Finish Limits") ([VictoryRoad](https://victoryroad.pro/2026-season-structure)). In TFT, finishing in the top half never loses ranked points ([Riot TFT FAQ](https://support.riotgames.com/tft/account/teamfight-tactics-ranked-faq)). Keep the 240 weekly cap, but make the League score the sum of a stable's best results that week, for example its best 20 finishes. A bad race then never hurts, extra entries help only a little, and the 3-minute race stays a chance to improve rather than a chore to farm *[inferred]*. The scarce resource does the rest. At the 3-starts-per-day maximum, a horse uses up its 40-start career in under two weeks, so deciding when to run each horse across a 4-week season is already a real strategic choice *[inferred]*.

Present the entry bank as an allowance, not an energy bar. The research found that the same numbers framed as a bonus feel rewarding, while a stamina bar that runs out feels like punishment ([Mobile Free to Play](https://mobilefreetoplay.com/?p=617)). The core shooter Midnight Star removed its energy system because good players disliked being told to stop and too few paid to skip it ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2015/05/28/core-gamers-and-f2p-energy-systems-dont-mix-says-developer-of-midnight-star)). Post Time's bank already allows banking, as TFT does for its top players. Earn the upgrades to 15/day and a 30-entry bank through Story licences or credits, never with cash or ETH. Paid entries would be paid chances at League points and prizes *[inferred]*. Sokker's rule that only players who played in a match during the week get trained shows how to tie development to taking part ([Sokker forum](https://sokker.org/forum_topic/ID_forum/24854/ID_topic/3616723/pg/0)). Post Time can do the same with form or fitness gains from racing, with the 3-per-day limit stopping it from turning into a grind *[inferred]*. Keep the Daily Cup as the day's single appointment: the same conditions for everyone and a daily leaderboard, so it plays like a daily puzzle *[inferred]*.

## Qualifying needs four ways in and a consolation final

The best qualifying systems in the research let many players try. They mix points built up over time with one-off races, offer several routes in, and pay something below the top spot. Pokémon's circuit gives automatic Worlds invites to event winners, who **don't take up places on the points list**, and fills the rest from zone rankings ([VictoryRoad](https://victoryroad.pro/2026-season-structure)). Hearthstone added a **consistency invite** (top 8 in six qualifiers in a season, later five) and applied it retroactively, which immediately qualified 24 players ([Hearthstone blog](https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-us/blog/22988583); [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/hearthstone/news/changes-hearthstone-masters-qualifiers-single-elim-tourneys)). Umamusume's monthly Champions Meeting is the closest horse-game model. Conditions are announced in advance and registration opens 3 days early. Two qualifying rounds each last 2 days, with up to 4 attempts a day, and it ends in a one-race final split into **Final A and Final B** ([umamusu.wiki](https://umamusu.wiki/Game:Champions_Meeting); [dbltap](https://www.dbltap.com/guides/umamusume-champions-meeting-pvp-mode-explained-schedule)). Siliconera called it "fantastic, infuriating PvP" and said players kept building new teams for the next cup despite painful near-misses ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/umamusume-champions-meeting-is-fantastic-infuriating-pvp/)).

For Post Time's weekdays, run each Listed qualifier across many fields during the day. Let a stable make up to two attempts per day, count its best result, and allow only one horse per stable in any one field, like a coupled entry in real racing. Give qualifier points by finishing position and count each stable's best 3 of 5 weekday results. That leaves four ways into Saturday's G1. Door one is a top-3 in any qualifier field, which already exists. Door two is a top-N finish on the week's qualifier points, with door-one qualifiers not taking those places. Door three is an official rating of 100+, which already exists. Door four is a consistency route, for example placing top-5 in qualifiers in three of the last four weeks. Next to the G1, run a **Saturday Plate**: a Listed-level consolation race for the stables that finished just outside qualifying. Umamusume's Final B does the same job, as does Hattrick's "Lucky Losers" rule, which lets some teams that just missed promotion fill vacancies ([Hattrick Series System](https://www8.hattrick.org/en-us/Help/Rules/SeriesSystem.aspx)). This gives the grind–qualify–fail–retry loop a soft landing, so a near-miss stable still has a Saturday race *[inferred]*.

Keep the finals short and decisive. Hearthstone switched to single-elimination qualifiers because its round-robin-style Swiss format "took too long", cutting events from about 12 hours to about 6 ([Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/hearthstone/news/changes-hearthstone-masters-qualifiers-single-elim-tourneys)). The Saturday Feature should be one broadcast race, or seeded heats with a final if the field is oversubscribed. Big races should also move ratings more: GT7 changes Driver Rating more after championship races than after Daily Races ([gran-turismo.com, Jan 2025](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/gt7/news/00_4914793.html)).

Near-miss feedback is powerful, so tell it honestly. In a Candy Crush study, near-misses were more arousing than losses and produced the strongest urge to keep playing of any outcome ([Larche et al., PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5445157/)). A systematic review found players rate near-misses as *more* unpleasant than plain losses ([J Gambling Studies](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-016-9633-7)), and the effect is stronger when players feel in control ([Clark et al.](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/368/1/mmc1.pdf)). Post Time should show real gaps, never manufacture them: "You finished 9th on qualifier points; 8 qualify; 4 points short." The replay should also show what cost the race, such as the training plan, a going mismatch or a poor draw. This uses the motivating effect of control without copying slot-machine design *[inferred]*.

## Four-week seasons reset the standings, never the stables

Post Time's 4-week season matches Hearthstone's monthly ladder reset more closely than Path of Exile's or Diablo's three-month leagues or TFT's four-month sets. Hearthstone resets the visible rank each month, keeps the hidden skill rating (MMR), and gives a **star-bonus multiplier** based on last season so strong players climb back quickly instead of crowding the lower tiers ([hearthstone.wiki.gg](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Ranked)). Fresh starts bring players back in waves. Diablo IV's leads said seasonal characters let newcomers "jump into the game without feeling left behind" ([Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/gaming/does-every-diablo-4-season-require-new-characters-blizzard-says-yes)), and Path of Exile's league launches set its player-count records, with lulls between them ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/mmo/path-exile-hit-new-all-time-high-steam-player-count-july-26-update); [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/238960/charts)).

Post Time's stables are the long-term investment, so the reset should hit standings, not horses. Reset season points and qualifier tables. Pull each stable's hidden rating part of the way toward the middle. Keep official horse ratings, the Hall of Fame and the Trophy Room untouched *[inferred]*. To give everyone an equal start without wiping collections, add a **restricted juvenile division** each season, open only to horses foaled that season, ending in its own Season Final. This is the Post Time version of Diablo's seasonal versus eternal realms *[inferred]*. Give each season one **featured condition**, such as a featured going or distance division, announced at season start, so the ideal bloodlines and training plans change on a timetable. Path of Exile and Diablo use new seasonal mechanics the same way, to shake up dominant builds ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/diablo-4/seasonal-mechanics-blizzard-interview)). That also addresses Umamusume's main pain point, where the whole field converged on one meta pick ("everyone's turned to Gold Ship") ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/umamusume-champions-meeting-is-fantastic-infuriating-pvp/)).

Season Finals should use the same multi-door entry as the weekly funnel. Weekend Feature winners get in automatically. The rest come from season points built on each stable's best three weeks and from a consistency route, and there should be A and B finals in each distance division. Players also need a longer goal above the season. Derby Stallion's mobile spin-off still runs an annual national championship qualified over three points races ([gamebiz](https://gamebiz.jp/news/418813)). One Photo Finish LIVE owner chased its monthly Virtual Kentucky Derby for about a year, finishing second twice before winning ([KentuckyDerby.com](https://www.kentuckyderby.com/horses/news/photo-finish-live-unlocks-a-new-generation-of-horse-racing-fans/)). Winning Post 10 lets successful sire lines earn "world bloodline" and, in the 2026 edition, "Founder lineage" status ([Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/games/winning-post-10-2026)). Post Time should group seasons into a **racing year** that ends in a year-end championship, and give bloodline honours to sire lines whose descendants win Features across several seasons. Players who breed for the long term then have a goal measured in months *[inferred]*. Finally, plan for return waves. Put announcements, a recap and a "new season starts Monday" hook at every 4-week boundary, and change competition rules only at season boundaries. Naavik found Zed Run's frequent mid-stream rule changes eroded trust ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)).

## Ladders need floors at the bottom and activity rules at the top

The best-regarded ladders have three layers. A **hidden skill rating** drives matchmaking. A **visible, forgiving progress layer** sits on top, with floors and no-loss bands. A small **apex layer** at the top has activity rules ([Riot TFT FAQ](https://support.riotgames.com/tft/account/teamfight-tactics-ranked-faq); [hearthstone.wiki.gg](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Ranked)). In Post Time, the per-horse official rating (0–140, with handicaps by rating band) is the horse's handicap and should stay that way. What's missing is a **hidden stable-level rating** used to seed League groups and draw qualifier fields. Glicko-2 suits Post Time's irregular play because its uncertainty grows when a player is away, so ratings recover quickly after a break ([Lichess FAQ](https://lichess.org/faq)) *[inferred fit]*.

Add floors to the weekly Leagues. Since patch 11.22, TFT players below Grandmaster cannot drop out of a tier. Hearthstone has floors at ranks 10 and 5 of every league ([Riot TFT FAQ](https://support.riotgames.com/tft/account/teamfight-tactics-ranked-faq); [hearthstone.wiki.gg](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Ranked)). Post Time should keep 5-up/5-down movement from Gold upward and remove relegation out of Bronze and Silver, so new stables are never pushed down while learning. New stables should start in provisional Bronze groups, padded with bot stables where needed, as Hattrick does in its lower divisions ([Hattrick Series System](https://www8.hattrick.org/en-us/Help/Rules/SeriesSystem.aspx)). Floors also reduce the urge to start a fresh account. Pokémon Showdown says its rating floor makes it "unnecessary to create new accounts to 'fix' your rating" ([PS ladder help](https://pokemonshowdown.com/pages/ladderhelp)).

At the top, make holding Champion tier depend on activity. TFT drains ranked points from idle Master-and-above players after 14 banked games run out ([Riot TFT FAQ](https://support.riotgames.com/tft/account/teamfight-tactics-ranked-faq)). Lichess requires a game in the last week to appear on its leaderboard ([Lichess FAQ](https://lichess.org/faq)). Post Time's Champion tier should require a minimum number of starts each week to keep its place, with a "lucky loser" from Platinum filling any vacancy. Champion should also show an absolute position number, like Hearthstone's Legend rank ([hearthstone.wiki.gg](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Legend)), because a visible "#14" is the trophy for players who have run out of tiers *[inferred]*. Season-end League rewards should require a minimum number of starts in the tier, in the same way Rocket League requires 10 wins at a rank to earn its reward ([Rocket League](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/competitive-season-1-title-rewards/)). That rewards sustained play rather than one lucky peak.

## Hard caps and horse exits, not price rises, stop breeding inflation

The research is clear about one failure mode. **Raising breeding prices does not stop horse inflation; removing animals does.** When Axie Infinity raised breeding costs about 66% in September 2021, daily births "did not significantly drop". Floor prices fell more than 75% in three months, and analysts' main proposed fix was a way to permanently remove Axies ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/axie-infinity-deconstruction)). Zed Run's horses "don't die or lose ability with age… no burn mechanism", so supply only grew ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)). Post Time already has the exits Zed Run lacked: a 40-start career, 4 lifetime breedings, the Legacy Farm and fatal injuries. Its genetics also pull foals back toward the average, since about half of good-by-good foals land below both parents. Keep all of these meaningful and add a **per-stable, per-season breeding cap**. Hard caps are more reliable than prices *[inferred from the Axie result]*.

Fix the purse problem now, during the beta. Live purses are about 3x what the economy model was balanced on. Axie's founder said of its late faucet cut, "This change should have been made much sooner" ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/92190/axie-infinity-making-big-changes-to-fix-its-ailing-play-to-earn-nft-economy)). Re-baselining in a closed beta, with the reason explained, costs far less goodwill than a nerf after launch *[inferred]*. To manage the economy afterwards, copy the long-lived games. EVE Online's developer publishes a **Monthly Economic Report** breaking down where its currency is created and destroyed, with raw data files ([MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/?p=50267); [EVE forums](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/monthly-economic-report-february-2021/302188)). Old School RuneScape charges a **2% sales tax** on its trading post (since 2023). Part of the tax funds a sink that buys oversupplied high-end items at market price and deletes them ([OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Update:Grand_Exchange_Tax_%26_Item_Sink); [RuneScape Wiki](https://runescape.wiki/w/Money_Sinks)). Hattrick uses weekly wages as a sink and steers its economy to be "not over-heated… [but] not starving" ([Hattrick rules](https://en-us.hattrick.org/Help/Rules/Economy.aspx); [FOFC archive](https://forums.operationsports.com/fofc/archive/index.php/t-59617.html)). For Post Time, that means a monthly report of credits created by source, credits destroyed by sink, horses born and horses removed, kept internal first and published once stable. Add a small market sales tax, part of which funds a Legacy Farm buyout of surplus horses, and a modest weekly upkeep per horse in training. The target is roughly one horse removed for every horse born *[inferred]*.

Two existing features need care. The appraisal floors and ceilings help against cheating, because they stop a second account passing credits to a main by "selling" a horse for almost nothing. But Naavik found Zed Run's mandatory minimum stud fees distorted its market and narrowed the meta ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)). Keep the band wide and let the monthly report decide whether to tighten it *[inferred]*. The bigger issue is that **premium foals can be traded for credits**, which lets money buy competitive power. The long-lived subscription games charge for convenience, status and extra clubs. Hattrick's Supporter tiers have been its main income since 2001 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattrick_(video_game)); [Hattrick Additional club](https://en-us.hattrick.org/Help/Rules/AdditionalClub.aspx)), and Star Stable's membership unlocks content and a weekly coin allowance ([Star Stable Help](https://help.starstable.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001373620-What-s-the-Difference-Between-a-Star-Rider-and-a-Non-Star-Rider)). (The research did not confirm in writing that Supporter has no effect on match results.) Before any prize money goes live, either tie premium foals to the buyer's stable or make Premium packs cosmetic and convenience-only. A paid route to stronger horses also weakens the "purchase does not improve your chances" position the giveaway depends on (see below) *[inferred]*.

## Integrity: verifiable seeds are a head start; identity is the gap

Post Time's committed seeds and verifiable replays already answer one of Zed Run's main criticisms: hidden stats and closed-server race resolution, which led players to suspect unannounced algorithm changes ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)). Promote that feature. The weak spot is identity. Chess.com closes **about 116,000–152,000 accounts a month** for fair-play violations ([Chess.com Fair Play, Jul 2025](https://www.chess.com/blog/FairPlay/fair-play-update-july-2025); [blog index](https://www.chess.com/blog/FairPlay)), and an older report found **nearly 40% of closed accounts were two weeks old or less** ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/chess-com-closes-100k-accounts-monthly-for-fair-play-violations/)). Valve banned about 90,000 Dota 2 smurf accounts in 2023, traced each one to its owner's main account, and warned that mains would be punished from then on ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/valve-banned-90000-smurf-accounts-from-dota-2then-got-the-main-accounts-too)).

Post Time's risks are specific: second stables feeding horses, credits or stud services to a main stable, groups sharing qualifier fields to block or shape results, and bots farming the 3-minute race *[inferred]*. Put these measures in place before prizes. Link stables by device, payment and wallet signals, and penalise the person rather than the account. Allow only one horse per stable in each qualifier field, and draw fields at random within rating bands from the committed seed so groups cannot choose to share a field. Flag horse sales between linked stables. Set minimum account age and activity before anyone is prize-eligible. Publish a penalty ladder. Riot's win-trading policy is a template: a first offence brings a 7-day ban, a reset to the lowest tier and loss of all season rewards, and a repeat offence a permanent ban ([Riot Wild Rift support](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/wild-rift/account/bannable-offenses-win-trading)). Repair the victims, as Lichess does by refunding up to 150 rating points to players who lost to a confirmed cheater ([Lichess FAQ](https://lichess.org/faq)). In Post Time, that means recomputing the affected week's qualifier points and Feature field. Include a review and appeal step. Riot publicly admitted a rushed TFT collusion ruling ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/teamfight-tactics/shitouren-banned-from-competitive-tft-riot-admits-mistake)). Write staff rules too: Zed Run had to compensate players after a team member bought horses on inside information ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)). Staff should be barred from prizes and from trading on unannounced changes. Best-of counting and the daily entry cap already cut what a bot can gain *[inferred]*.

## Spend the $1,000 on one free, season-long Founders' ladder

The law-firm guidance points to one structure. **Make entry free, fund the prizes yourself, fix every prize before entries open, and rank on skill over a long period.** A prize funded by an operator who does not compete, and that doesn't depend on how many people enter, is treated very differently from a pool built out of entry fees ([Fasthoff Law](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/skill-contests-versus-sweepstakes-entry-fees)). Florida courts have allowed skill games where the prize is announced in advance, is not made of entry fees and is not competed for by the operator ([Walters Law Group](https://www.firstamendment.com/list-states-skill-gaming-allowed-prohibited/)). A $1,000 total sits below the **$5,000 total-prize threshold** that triggers registration and bonding in New York and Florida ([Fasthoff Law](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/sweepstakes-state-registration-bonding)). Every individual prize also sits below the **$2,000 US 1099-MISC threshold** that applies from 2026, though prizes are still taxable income for winners ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102ku4y/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-could-mean-more-valuable-prizes/); [Verrill](https://www.verrill-law.com/blog/the-new-2000-threshold-for-sending-irs-form-1099-misc-to-prize-winners/)). Avoid random draws. A draw among top scorers adds an element of chance and brings in sweepstakes rules, including a free method of entry ([Fasthoff Law](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/skill-contests-versus-sweepstakes-entry-fees)). This is a summary of the legal landscape, not legal advice.

The plan: run a **Founders' Season** over one full 4-week season, after the competitive loop has been tuned. Rank stables on Founders' points from their best three Weekend Feature weeks plus their Season Final finish. Pay **1st $400, 2nd $250, 3rd $150 and 4th–8th $40 each**, announced before the season starts, with a published tie-breaker based on a skill measure such as best Season Final finish, then season points *[inferred split, following the research notes]*. Add free, non-cash recognition for breadth: a Hall of Fame plaque, a Trophy Room item, a race named after the winning horse next season, and a cosmetic for every stable that completes a set number of starts. Eligibility should be 18+, one stable per person, staff excluded, restricted places excluded, and the account registered and active before the season starts. Pay in fiat or gift cards rather than USDC, and check ID only for winners before payout, which is how Sorare handles rewards ([Sorare Help](https://help.sorare.com/hc/en-us/articles/35396884763549-Identity-Verification)). Keep scoring and disqualification records. California requires disclosures and two years of records for contests ([Fasthoff Law](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/skill-contests-versus-sweepstakes-entry-fees)). Before launch, deal with the premium-foal trade, or exclude premium-origin horses from Founders' points, so no purchase improves a player's chances *[inferred]*.

Expect prize farmers, and judge success by retention. A study of crypto airdrops found that **73–87% of eligible accounts claimed on day one**, 48–66% of tokens went straight to exchanges, the median recipient made one transfer and was active for zero days, and one farmer reportedly ran over 20,000 accounts ([Messias, Yaish & Livshits](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02752v4)). Players motivated by earnings stay like it's a job. Axie's 25–30% 30-day retention was described as "the retention rate of a job, not a game" ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/axie-infinity-deconstruction)). Measure the Founders' Season by entrants' 30-day retention after it ends, not by sign-ups. Requiring sustained activity before the season starts screens out most farmers, at the cost of some casual players ([TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/20762)).

## USDC markets and paid-entry races repeat Zed Run's losing bet

The recommendation is **no to paid-entry USDC prize races and no to a USDC-priced horse market** in the current design. Zed Run is the direct comparison. It had 12-horse paid races with entry fees of $2–$500 and a 60/25/15 split, so **9 of 12 entrants lost money in a typical race**. Even a top racer won about $15,000 across 578 races against about $25,000 in entry fees. Naavik called paid racing zero-sum like poker: it lasts only while losing players keep paying, and when they leave the skilled players go too ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/dec-2021-zed-run/)). Zed Run peaked at 8–10k daily active owners, stalled from September 2021 and shut down on 28 February 2025. Horse NFTs did not carry over, holders called it a "rug", and trading volume fell from about $28,000 to $627 in a month ([BlockchainGamer.biz](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/37092/zed-run-discontinued-for-zed-champions/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/316789/ethereum-nft-game-zed-run-reborn-base)). Axie shows the economic mechanism: an economy that pays cash depends on new money coming in, and when growth slowed its daily users fell from 2.7M toward 1.5M within months ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/92190/axie-infinity-making-big-changes-to-fix-its-ailing-play-to-earn-nft-economy); [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2022/axie-infinity-hack/)).

The legal landscape is also hostile. US states test for chance in different ways. Most use a "dominant factor" test, but New York's "material degree" test catches games where skill matters but chance still plays a meaningful part ([Fasthoff Law](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/skill-contests-versus-sweepstakes-entry-fees)). Post Time's races include seeded randomness, which is hard to square with that test *[inferred]*. Real-money skill platforms such as Skillz block roughly 5–12 US states ([Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6452588929); [terms.law](https://terms.law/Terms-Scanner/app/gaming/skills-based-platforms/skillz/)). In the UK, Sorare, a heavily funded company with lawyers, was charged in 2024 with providing unlicensed gambling facilities after a roughly three-year investigation. It denies the charge, and its trial has been postponed to June 2027 ([BlockchainGamer.biz](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/34595/uk-gambling-commission-charges-sorare/)). France's new trial framework for games with cash-value digital items (JONUM) allows racing and breeding games, but requires identity checks, spending and time limits that players set themselves, self-exclusion, and reward caps ([SoloAzar](https://www.soloazar.com/en/category/legislation/france-approves-three-year-trial-decree-for-monetisable-digital-object-games)). Commercially, Sorare's revenue fell from about €143M (2022) to €43M (2024) ([ReadWrite](https://readwrite.com/nft-sports-card-collecting-and-betting-platform-sorare-reportedly-on-the-verge-of-collapse/)). A USDC horse market would also make Premium-pack horses cashable, which makes a paid random pack look like the paid loot boxes Belgium ruled illegal gambling in 2018 ([Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/5801/games-developers-remove-paid-loot-boxes-in-belgium)) *[inferred link]*. Both ideas break the project's own economy rules: Stable Credits never cashed out, no prize money, no gambling mechanics.

The strongest case for the idea deserves an answer. Real stakes do attract competitive players. Sorare reportedly grew active cardholders 33% to 378,000 even while cutting 35% of staff ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2025/11/21/sorare-lays-off-35-of-staff-cto-steps-back-as-nft-boom-fades/)), and Photo Finish LIVE combines real-money play with an official Kentucky Derby partnership ([KentuckyDerby.com](https://www.kentuckyderby.com/horses/news/photo-finish-live-unlocks-a-new-generation-of-horse-racing-fans/)). Yet DappRadar puts Photo Finish at only "hundreds" of daily active wallets ([DappRadar](https://dappradar.com/dapp/photo-finish-live)). The real-money horse games stayed small or collapsed, while the free-to-play ones (Umamusume at about $2.8bn lifetime mobile revenue, Hattrick at about 200,000 users for more than five years) grew or lasted ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/umamusume-pretty-derby-races-to-100m-on-mobile-in-one-year-overseas/); [Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.ch/en/page/hattrick-you-can-easily-play-this-game-for-ten-or-twenty-years-31883)). If the owner wants to reconsider later, set conditions first: 30-day retention in the top quarter without money, a monthly economy report showing horses removed at about the rate they are born, and a written legal opinion for each target market. Even then, use **sponsor-funded, fixed-prize events with free entry** (the Founders' model, scaled up), never pools built from entry fees. Use the chain for proof rather than payouts: publish seed commitments on-chain and issue non-transferable trophies *[inferred]*.

## Geeky players stay for the calculator, the stud book and the meta report

The players most likely to stay for years want to plan and to build something that lasts. Quantic Foundry's data on more than 1.25M gamers finds that **Strategy is the motivation that changes least with age**, and its "Architect" profile wants progression, strategic decisions and to "build something that lasts" ([Quantic Foundry GDC 2019](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Quantic-Foundry-GDC-2019-Talk.pdf)). Japanese breeding sims show how far that appetite goes. Winning Post 10 2026's manual lists dozens of named mating theories, including inbreeding variants, single to "force" nicks, line-breeding, outcrosses, dosage and the Rasmussen factor. The mating screen has a "danger" indicator for risky crosses ([WP10 2026 manual](https://www.gamecity.ne.jp/manual/WP102026/jp/7300.html); [gameranbu](https://gameranbu.jp/winningpost9/911f96e8b9ab3ff0c3c2)). Pokémon's competitive scene grew around tools that make a deep system calculable: a free simulator, monthly usage statistics and an open-source damage calculator ([PS ladder](https://pokemonshowdown.com/ladder/gen9vgc2025regi); [smog-usage-stats](https://pypi.org/project/smog-usage-stats/1.0.3)). Hattrick's approved data access (CHPP) supports Hattrick Organizer, an open-source helper still being released. It allows a 16-week training plan but only a two-match look at opponents ([Hattrick wiki](https://wiki.hattrick.org/wiki/Hattrick_Organizer); [GitHub](https://github.com/akasolace/HO/releases)).

Post Time's in-game wiki with live rule numbers is a strong base. Build five things on it *[inferred]*. First, a **mating report** that names inbreeding level, fragile-legs carrier odds and the expected spread of foal quality, so "half of good-by-good foals land below both parents" appears as a chart rather than a surprise. Second, a **race calculator** that shows a win-probability range for two horses at a given distance and going. Third, a **monthly meta report** on Feature entrants and winners by sire line, running style and training plan. Fourth, a **read-only data export or API** with a delay on opponent data. Fifth, unrewarded **exhibition races against ghost horses**, so breeders can test a plan before spending weeks on it. Content cadence still matters as much as tools. Umamusume's overseas mobile revenue fell from $22.5M in month two to $10.4M in month three, and its biggest spike came from a story update with a new featured card, not from its feature film ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/umamusume-pretty-derby-races-to-100m-on-mobile-in-one-year-overseas/)). Star Stable has shipped weekly updates since 2011 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/rise-star-stable-online/)). The CEO of the long-running football manager Top Eleven admitted its team had neglected the core game and had to refocus on it ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/live-and-kicking-nordeus-top-eleven-ten-years-on/)).

## Conclusion

Most of Post Time's competitive design is already right: scarce starts, horses that age and retire, results anyone can verify, and a weekly-then-monthly rhythm that matches the genre's longest-running games. The main finding is that its weak points are the **connections between those layers**. A Live race should say which week it serves, a failed qualifier should lead somewhere, a season should change what is worth breeding, and a breeding program should earn honours that outlast it. Fix those connections and the grind gets its meaning from the game, not from the prize.

The second finding is about sequence. Prize money, and later any real-money feature, magnifies whatever loop it is attached to. Paid competitive power, invisible multi-accounting or an over-generous economy then become the things players fight over. Retune purses, deal with premium foals and add identity checks first. Then the $1,000 rewards the game the owner actually wants. The project's existing rule against cashing out credits then reads as a competitive advantage: it lets Post Time keep the prestige of a ladder without the zero-sum economics that ended Zed Run.
