# Syndicates (design, 2026-10-31)

From the go-to-market plan: groups that co-own horses and race under shared silks, a social reason to
bring friends. **Credits only.** No USDG, T-USDG or anything bought with money, until a separate
legal review of shared ownership.

## What a syndicate is

- A named group of 2-6 stables with its own silks and a manager (the founder; can hand over).
- It owns horses through **shares** (10 shares per horse). Members buy shares with credits when the
  syndicate buys or breeds a horse; the horse races under the syndicate's silks and name.
- **Purses** are split by shares automatically at settlement; **costs** (training, upkeep, entry fees,
  extras, jockey fees, vet) are split the same way and charged to each member's balance, with the
  manager choosing the plan (entries, tactics, training) within a weekly budget the members agreed.
- A horse needs a stall: the syndicate rents one stall per horse from its members' spare stalls
  (a member lends a stall and gets a small fee), so syndicates don't bypass stall limits.

## Rules that keep it fair

- A stable can be in at most 2 syndicates and hold at most 5 shares in any one horse.
- Linked accounts can't be in the same syndicate (market anti-alt rules).
- Shares can be sold to other members or listed to non-members at a price; the market's resale lock and
  fees apply.
- Breeding a syndicate horse: decided by member vote (majority of shares); foals belong to the
  syndicate.
- Leaving: sell your shares, or the syndicate buys them back at appraisal minus 10 % after a 7-day
  notice.
- Rivals, news, the form book, finances and the tote all treat the syndicate as the runner's
  connections: no syndicate member may bet on a race where the syndicate has a runner.

## Phasing

1. Syndicate, silks, membership, shares, purse and cost split, manager plan, votes on breeding.
2. Share trading, stall lending, syndicate pages and leaderboards, "bring friends" invites.
3. Only after legal review: anything involving money.
